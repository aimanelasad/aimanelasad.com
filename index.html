About me

I'm an Industrial AI researcher with a background in physics, mathematics, software engineering and automotive production. From 2022 to 2026 I carried out my doctoral research at Mercedes-Benz AG in Sindelfingen within the SofDCar project, in cooperation with the University of Stuttgart. My dissertation, AI-Based Software Update Release Impact Analysis, develops methods that estimate how a vehicle software update will affect production before it is rolled out. I passed my doctoral examination in July 2026 with the distinction magna cum laude.

My work sits at the intersection of these topics:

  • Software-defined vehicles — release documentation, commissioning and testing data, software traceability.
  • Production quality — First Time Quality (FTQ) as a measurable indicator for the impact of software updates.
  • Large language models — retrieval-augmented generation, agentic systems and semantic clustering of production error messages.

Before moving into industrial AI I studied physics at RWTH Aachen University (B.Sc. in experimental, M.Sc. in theoretical particle physics) alongside additional studies in mathematics, and I taught theoretical physics to bachelor's and master's students for three years. I still enjoy teaching: in 2026 I spent a semester as a physics and mathematics teacher at a Gymnasium.

Outside of work I like cooking, art and photography, plants and reading.

Projects

A selection of projects from my research and my spare time.

AI-based release impact analysis

Doctoral research project: a data pipeline and LLM-based analysis that links vehicle software release documentation to commissioning and production-quality data, so that the production impact of an update can be estimated before rollout. Published in Procedia CIRP and as SAE papers.

Related publications

First Time Quality as an update-impact indicator

Statistical evaluation of First Time Quality (FTQ) across software releases, turning a plant-level quality metric into a quantitative signal for update impact. Presented at the 2026 Stuttgart International Symposium.

SAE 2026-01-0786

Semantic clustering of production error messages

An LLM-embedding approach that groups free-text error messages from vehicle commissioning into semantically coherent clusters to speed up root-cause analysis. Poster at AmEC 2026.

gpu-kernel-lab

A hands-on CUDA optimisation lab: stencil, reduction, softmax and SGEMM kernels are tuned step by step and measured against a roofline model (SGEMM reaching 97 % of cuBLAS on an RTX A2000). Each step documents the hypothesis, the measurement and — where it failed — why.
